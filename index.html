HONG KONG
▼
Symphony of LightsANCHOR — Oct 9 & Oct 12
Free nightly laser and light show at 8pm from the TST waterfront promenade. The entire HK Island skyline lights up with synchronised music and lasers sweeping the harbour for 15 minutes. One of the best free shows anywhere in the world. Arrive by 7:45pm for a good spot.
From Holiday Inn Golden Mile: 10-min walk eastTime needed: 30 minBest day: Oct 9 or Oct 12
The Peak & Peak TramANCHOR — Oct 9
Victoria Peak via the historic Peak Tram — one of the steepest funicular railways in the world. The views over HK Island and Kowloon are extraordinary. Book tram tickets in advance at thepeak.com.hk to avoid long walk-up queues. Free viewing at Peak Galleria; paid Sky Terrace 428 for elevated views.
From hotel: MTR to Central then 10-min walk, ~25 min totalTime needed: 2–3 hrsBest day: Oct 9
Kowloon Walled City ParkANCHOR — Oct 8
A peaceful, beautifully landscaped park on the site of the former Kowloon Walled City — one of the most densely populated places in human history. The exhibition hall inside tells the full story with maps, photographs and scale models. Completely flat, entirely stroller-friendly.
From hotel: MTR Kwun Tong Line to Lok Fu, 10-min walk south, ~25 min totalTime needed: 1.5–2 hrsBest day: Oct 8
Mong Kok MarketsANCHOR — Oct 11
The Flower Market Road, Goldfish Market (Tung Choi Street North), and Ladies Market (Tung Choi Street South) form a continuous flat walkable strip through one of Kowloon's most characterful neighbourhoods. Cantonese bargaining is the sport here. Langham Place for a nursing room break.
From hotel: MTR to Mong Kok, ~5 minTime needed: Half dayBest day: Oct 11
Sham Shui Po
One of HK's most local and undervisited neighbourhoods. Apliu Street flea market (old electronics, cameras, gadgets), fabric and trimming markets on Ki Lung Street, and excellent cheap dai pai dong lunch spots throughout. Completely flat, very stroller-friendly.
From Kowloon Walled City Park: MTR ~10 minTime needed: 2–3 hrsBest day: After Oct 8 anchor
1881 Heritage
The former Marine Police Headquarters beautifully converted into a heritage precinct with luxury shops and restaurants. Free to wander the colonial-era buildings and grounds. Very close to the hotel and the waterfront.
From Holiday Inn Golden Mile: 10-min walkTime needed: 30–45 minBest day: Any HK day
HK Museum of History
100 Chatham Road South, TST. One of the best city history museums in Asia — the permanent exhibition covers 400 million years of HK history from geology to the 1997 handover. Stroller-friendly inside. Air conditioned — excellent on a warm afternoon.
From hotel: 10-min walkTime needed: 1.5–2 hrsBest day: Any HK day

JAPANESE — ESSENTIAL
Mayo nashi de onegaishimasuマヨネーズなしでお願いします
No mayonnaise please — Becca's critical phrase
🚨 Say before ordering ANYTHING in Japan. Kewpie mayo is hidden in: takoyaki, okonomiyaki, convenience store sandwiches, potato salad, many sauces. Non-negotiable.
Sumimasenすみません
Excuse me / Sorry — use constantly
Opens every door. Say before asking anyone anything.
Arigatou gozaimasuありがとうございます
Thank you (formal)
Say with a slight bow. People genuinely light up.
Junyuushitsu wa arimasu ka?授乳室はありますか？
Is there a nursing room?
Every department store has one. Staff will lead you immediately. Look for 赤ちゃん休憩室 signs.
Kore wa ikura desu ka?これはいくらですか？
How much is this?
Point at the item. Works anywhere.
Toire wa doko desu ka?トイレはどこですか？
Where is the toilet?
Japan has the best toilets on earth. Ask freely.
Watashi wa tounyoubyou desu私は糖尿病です
I am diabetic — Goong Goong's card phrase
Print on a small card. Show at restaurants and clinics. Japanese medical staff will respond immediately and helpfully.
Kuroge Wagyu / A5黒毛和牛 / A5
Black wagyu / highest grade — look for on restaurant windows
Walk in and ask for the lunch teishoku set. Best value of the trip.
Kusushon wa arimasu ka?クッションはありますか？
Do you have a cushion? (for Jem at restaurants)
Most Japanese restaurants have booster cushions for babies. Just ask.
CANTONESE — ESSENTIAL
Màh-gōi唔該

HK: use Cantonese. Shenzhen: Mandarin is the norm.

MONEY
→Tax-free shopping Japan: Purchases over ¥5,500 (~CAD $52) get 10% back. Bring passports everywhere. Works at Don Quijote, Uniqlo, Muji, Loft, Hands, most department stores. Present passport at the tax-free counter.
→Hong Kong: Octopus Card for all transit. Buy at HKG arrivals. Cards accepted everywhere.
→Shenzhen: Cashless — WeChat Pay essential. Set up and test before crossing. Download WeChat and link a Canadian credit card beforehand.
→Japan: Cash still widely used. Carry ¥30,000 (~CAD $285) at all times. 7-Eleven ATMs accept international cards reliably.
→Suica: Load on Apple/Google Wallet before landing. Covers all Japan trains, convenience stores, and vending machines. Tap to pay everywhere.
→JR Pass: Calculate value at japan-guide.com/e/e2361.html before buying. Nozomi Shinkansen NOT covered — use Hikari if on a pass.
KONBINI (CONVENIENCE STORES) ★
→7-Eleven, FamilyMart, and Lawson in Japan are extraordinary — nothing like back home. Hot food, fresh onigiri, beautiful sandwiches, pastries, ramen, hot soup, beer, sake, wine.
→For Jem: Merries diapers (メリーズ) available at virtually every konbini — the best diaper in the world. Formula, baby food, wet wipes all stocked.
→Services: ATM (7-Eleven accepts international cards), Suica top-up, luggage forwarding (takuhaibin), package pickup, ticket printing, photocopying.
→There is a konbini within 50 metres of wherever you are at all times in Japan. Use them constantly. You will spend a lot of time in these.
VENDING MACHINES (自動販売機)
→Japan has 5 million vending machines. Hot coffee, cold coffee, cold drinks, hot soup, green tea, beer, snacks, umbrellas — all from machines on every corner.
→BOSS canned coffee (hot) and Georgia canned coffee are iconic. ¥130–160 (~CAD $1.25–1.50). The hot can in your hands on a cold Kyoto morning is a genuine pleasure.
→If you want a drink, look left or right — there is a machine within 50 metres.
LUGGAGE FORWARDING (宅配便 / TAKUHAIBIN)
→Send bags between hotels overnight — ~¥1,500–2,500 (~CAD $14–24) per bag. Arrives next day before you do.
→Essential on Oct 21 evening: Send heavy bags from MIMARU Kyoto to Keio Plaza Tokyo before the Oct 22 Shinkansen. Travel light on the train.
→Before Oct 30: Send large/heavy bags to Haneda Airport (HND) on Oct 29 — they arrive before you do on Oct 30 departure day.
→Book at hotel front desk or any konbini. Yamato Transport (黒猫) and Sagawa are the main services. Japan Post also works.

🛁 ONSEN ETIQUETTE
→Shower first — always: Thoroughly wash your entire body at the shower station before entering the bath. This is non-negotiable. Shampoo, soap, rinse completely. The bath is for soaking, not cleaning.
→No towels in the water: Your small modesty towel stays outside the bath or folded on your head — never in the water. Most onsen provide two towels: a large one for drying after, a small one for modesty walking around.
→Enter slowly: The water is hot (40–43°C standard). Lower yourself in gradually. Your body adjusts within a minute or two. Don't splash or swim — still soaking only.
→Time limit: 15–20 minutes maximum per session, especially at natural hot spring temperature. Drink water before and after. Dizziness on standing up is common — stand slowly.
→Tattoos: Public onsen baths in Japan typically prohibit tattoos. The private family room (家族風呂 / Kazoku Buro) at Hinode Hotel has no such rule — you are the only guests in the room.
→What's provided: Hinode Hotel provides towels, soap, shampoo, and conditioner in the private room. No need to bring anything except yourselves and Jem.
→After the onsen: Pat dry gently — don't rub. The mineral water leaves a light coating on skin that moisturises naturally. Drink water. You will sleep extremely well.
→For Jem: See the dedicated onsen callout in the Jem tab — cool water to 37–38°C, maximum 10 minutes, rinse after. The private room makes all of this easy to manage.

→Kewpie baby food range (キューピーベビーフード): Not related to the mayo — completely separate product line. Japan's most trusted baby food brand. Purees, pouches, and jarred foods for every stage. Available at every konbini, supermarket, and drugstore. Rice porridge (okayu / おかゆ) for 5-month-olds and up is a staple first food.
→Wakodo (和光堂) is the other major baby food brand — also excellent, widely available, good for purees and dissolving crackers (boro / ボーロ) that Jem can gum.
→What to look for on packaging: 5ヶ月 = from 5 months, 7ヶ月 = from 7 months. The month indicator is always on the front of the package. Google Translate camera works perfectly on baby food labels.
→Bring from Canada: Enough for the first 2–3 days of solids plus a preferred formula buffer. After that, buy Kewpie or Wakodo locally — better variety and significantly cheaper than Canadian equivalents.
→At restaurants: Japanese staff are extraordinarily helpful with babies. Showing a small bowl and miming feeding will get you warm water for heating pouches, a cushion for Jem to sit on, and general delight. Just ask.
